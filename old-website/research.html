CORE RESEARCH PILLARS

What we study

Our work spans atomic-scale defect physics to engineering-scale data management — connecting fundamental materials science to the challenges facing nuclear industry today.

Radiation Damage in Structural Materials

Neutron and ion bombardment displaces atoms from their lattice positions, creating defects — voids, dislocation loops, precipitates — that progressively harden, embrittle, and swell metals over years of reactor service. We use ion-beam irradiation as a controlled, reactor-free proxy to build predictive degradation models.

RPV Steel Irradiation Database

Post-irradiation examination data accumulates worldwide yet remains fragmented, unstandardized, and difficult to interpret — leading to repeated experiments because relevant data is inaccessible. We are developing the Reactor Pressure Vessel (RPV) Steel Irradiation Database: a curated, large-scale repository enabling meaningful data mining across historical campaigns to support reactor safety.

Small-Scale & Post-Irradiation Mechanical Testing

Highly radioactive specimens demand entirely new methodologies. We develop nanoindentation, micropillar compression, and micro-tensile methods that extract full mechanical datasets from samples no larger than a grain of sand — reducing dose exposure while generating richer data than conventional bulk testing.

nanoindentation micropillar micro-tensile PIE

Liquid Metal Corrosion & Compatibility

Lead-bismuth eutectic and other heavy liquid metals are candidate coolants for advanced fission and accelerator-driven systems. We map corrosion kinetics, oxygen-driven attack, and liquid metal embrittlement in structural steels — generating the data material designers need for lifetime predictions.

LBE corrosion kinetics embrittlement ADS

Advanced Structural Materials Development

Conventional steels cannot meet the combined demands of future fission, fusion, and space reactor environments. We evaluate ODS alloys, SiC/SiC composites, high-entropy alloys, and refractory systems using accelerated protocols that compress decades of service into weeks of experimentation.

General Materials Science & Cross-Domain Applications

The experimental methods we develop for nuclear environments translate powerfully across industry. We apply our expertise in mechanical testing, microstructural characterization, and degradation science to coatings, surface engineering, semiconductors, oil and gas, and concentrated solar systems.

METHODOLOGY

Our experimental approach

Every accelerated method we develop is benchmarked against reactor-irradiated specimens from national laboratory partners — ensuring results transfer reliably from the lab bench to real nuclear environments.

Our work is made possible through strong partnerships with Los Alamos National Laboratory, Idaho National Laboratory, Argonne National Laboratory, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and the Paul Scherrer Institute in Switzerland.

FACILITY ACKNOWLEDGEMENT

We gratefully acknowledge the support of the Materials Characterization Facility (MCF) at UC Berkeley, whose state-of-the-art instrumentation underpins much of our microstructural and mechanical characterization work.

01

Ion-Beam Irradiation

We use ion accelerators to simulate neutron damage in days rather than years — enabling rapid, controlled screening of new material candidates without needing reactor access.

02

Miniaturized Mechanical Testing

Nanoindentation, micropillar compression, and micro-tensile loading — purpose-built for nanogram-scale irradiated specimens with minimal radiological handling requirements.

03

Atom Probe & Multi-Scale Characterization

APT, TEM, SEM/EBSD, and synchrotron X-ray techniques spanning atomic defect clusters all the way to engineering component behavior.

04

National Laboratory Validation

Access to neutron-irradiated benchmark specimens from LANL, INL, ANL, ORNL, and PSI anchors our accelerated test data to reactor-validated ground truth.

ADDITIONAL TOPICS

Further research areas

Beyond our six core pillars, we pursue supporting research themes that extend or apply our capabilities to new problems.

// 05
Coatings & Surface Modification
Protective coating systems extend component lifetimes in corrosive and irradiating environments. We characterize adhesion, phase stability, and long-term performance under ion-beam exposure and thermal cycling through accelerated aging protocols.
// 06
Isotopic & Hydride Effects in Alloys
Hydrogen isotopes dissolved in metals form hydride phases that alter atom probe measurement accuracy and local mechanical behavior. We quantify these effects in nuclear-relevant alloy systems to improve data fidelity.
// 07
High-Throughput Materials Screening
Novel macro- and micro-scale machining capabilities for pre- and post-irradiation examination that dramatically increase evaluation throughput — enabling faster iteration across larger alloy and coating design spaces.
APPLICATION DOMAINS
Where our research applies

The science we develop for nuclear environments transfers powerfully to any industry where materials must perform at their physical limits.

// 01

Fission Reactors

// 02

Fusion Systems

First-wall and breeding blanket materials under 14 MeV neutron bombardment and simultaneous helium co-implantation.

// 03

Spallation Targets

Materials for accelerator-driven systems and neutron user facilities operating in liquid-metal environments.

// 04

Beyond Nuclear

Oil & gas, concentrated solar, semiconductor fabrication, and aerospace — wherever extreme-environment science applies.
